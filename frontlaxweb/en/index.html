What you need and nothing else

It opens instantly

Press the shortcut and the selection is already there, in 28 milliseconds. No loading screen and no full second staring at a frozen desktop.

It takes up what an icon does

33 MB of memory while capturing and a 2.50 MB installer. The app Windows ships spends eight times more to do less.

Pixel exact magnifier

6× zoom with the colour in hex under the cursor, and windows highlight themselves with their name on them: one click and you have that whole window, with no fiddling with edges.

Three screens, one key

Turn on whole screen and every monitor puts up its own number. Press the number and it takes that one entire, wherever the pointer happens to be.

The last few seconds

It records all the time and drops the old, so one key keeps the last 15, 30 or 60 seconds. The good part of a screen almost always happens before anyone thinks of recording it.

Speed on export

0.5×, 2× or 4× over what you recorded. A tutorial has stretches where nothing happens, and those play at 2× without losing a thing.

Numbered steps

Click to drop a 1, a 2 and a 3 on the shot. They number themselves, so adding one in the middle does not leave two threes in the same picture.

GIF and video, not just stills

Record the region at 15, 30 or 60 frames and export to GIF or MP4. With no bundled FFmpeg: Windows writes the video in hardware.

Frame by frame editor

Trim the start and the end with two marks, watch the loop, change the size and the quality, and export. All in the same window.

With sound

The system audio and your microphone, mixed into the same track. Each has its own switch, and either can go alone: narrate a tutorial, or capture only what the machine is playing.

The camera moves in by itself

It zooms in on every click of the recording, and how much you decide after recording: the clicks were written down while it recorded, so changing your mind never means recording again.

It never leaves your machine

No account, no telemetry and no uploads. The code is open and the installer does not even ask for administrator rights.

Ask on GitHub

Is winshotx free?

Yes, all of it. It's open source under the MIT licence: the full code is on GitHub, with no time limit and no feature locked behind a payment.

Does it need an account or an internet connection?

No. It never asks for an account, sends nothing to any cloud, and works offline; the only moment it talks to the internet is to check for a new version.

Which versions of Windows does it run on?

Windows 10 and Windows 11.

Is the app available in English?

Yes. It follows your Windows language on its own, and in Settings you can force Spanish or English whenever you like. Every label, every explanation and the welcome screen are translated.

How do you install and update it?

The installer is 2.50 MB and goes to your user, not the system, so Windows never asks for administrator rights. It updates itself from inside the app with one button, and every download is signed and checked before anything is installed.

Can it be installed with winget?

Yes. It is published in the official winget-pkgs catalogue, so winget install Mun1to.winshotx installs it, and winget upgrade updates it. A new version takes a few hours to show up in the catalogue: while it does, the app updates itself from Settings. winget upgrade updates it.

What can it capture and record?

Region capture with automatic window snapping and a whole screen with one key; video and GIF at 15, 30 or 60 fps with system audio and microphone; the last 15, 30 or 60 seconds without pressing record beforehand; and a frame by frame editor with A and B trim, six marks to annotate, a mosaic, numbered steps, camera zoom on every click and export at 0.5×, 2× or 4×.

What does it still lack compared to the Windows Snipping Tool?

Three things. It does not come preinstalled: you have to install it. It does not black out personal data on its own, you cover it by hand with the mosaic. And it speaks two languages, Spanish and English, while the Windows one speaks all of them. The point by point comparison is above, including where winshotx loses.
